Agriculture Innovation Challenge · Problem Statement 4

DrainSmart

A closed-loop service that detects blocked field drains before heavy rain, sends the right crew, supervises the clearing, and uses sensors to confirm that water is flowing again.

Team: [Team name]Institution: [Institution]Pilot site (modelled): Amalapuram, Konaseema, Andhra Pradesh

1Summary

Debris such as water hyacinth, plastic and silt chokes culverts and trash screens on village drains. Nobody notices until water tops the bank, and by then the storm has arrived. DrainSmart puts two low-cost water-level sensors at each culvert. It compares their readings with a digital twin, a physics model of how a clear drain would respond to the same rain. Drains are ranked by flood risk, crews are alerted in their own language, and each task closes only when the sensors show normal flow.

We built a working prototype: a web control room driven by a hydraulic simulation of a six-node, 131-acre paddy drain, with the live rainfall forecast for the pilot village. In the reference heavy-storm scenario:

20 minfrom debris starting to collect to the first alert
45 minfrom detection to the drain verified clear
~200acre-hours of waterlogging avoided vs. no action
0false alerts in a 75 mm storm with no blockage

These figures come from simulation, not field measurement. The field pilot in section 11 is designed to replace them with measured values.

2Problem and context

Paddy in deltaic regions depends on dense networks of field drains that carry rainwater to rivers and outfalls. Three things make blockages costly:

  • Blockages build up silently. Floating weed and plastic collect at culverts and screens over hours or days. Nobody patrols every drain every day.
  • Rain gives little warning. When a blocked drain meets heavy rain, the water upstream reaches bank level within hours and spills into the fields. Prolonged submergence at the panicle stage damages yield, and water over the road cuts village access.
  • Responsibility is unclear. Shared drains fall between individual farmers, the gram panchayat and the drainage department, so a blockage often has no one assigned to it.

3Solution: a closed loop

The problem statement asks for a closed-loop service rather than a standalone sensor dashboard. DrainSmart has six stages, and every task passes through all of them.

1 · SENSEUltrasonic level sensors upstream and downstream of each culvert, rainfall from a gauge plus forecast, optional camera at the trash screen.
2 · DETECTThe digital twin predicts the level a clear drain would show. Water backing up above that prediction raises the Blockage Index.
3 · PRIORITISERisk combines blockage likelihood, rain due in the next 12 h, how close the water is to the bank, and acres exposed.
4 · ALERTSMS and voice alerts in Telugu, Hindi or English to the village crew, escalating to the panchayat if nobody accepts.
5 · CLEARA supervised rake behind a safety checklist and emergency stop at fitted culverts. Manual crews elsewhere.
6 · VERIFYThe task closes only after three consecutive normal readings. If verification fails, it reopens automatically.

4Detection method

The physical signature

A culvert behaves like a constriction. When debris narrows it, flow through it drops for the same upstream head, so water stores up upstream while the downstream channel stays flat or falls. This difference is easy to measure with one sensor on each side.

Why a digital twin is needed

Comparing upstream with downstream alone is not enough. Rain raises levels everywhere, and at an outfall the downstream level is fixed by the river. In our first engine test, a storm with no blockage made the outfall node (D6) look fully blocked. The fix is a twin model: a copy of the drain hydraulics that receives the same measured rain but never gets blocked. Detection works on residuals (observed minus twin) instead of raw levels.

r_up = upstream_observed − upstream_twin r_down = downstream_observed − downstream_twin excess = (r_up − r_down) − learned_offset # offset adapts slowly while BI < 0.2 diverge = slope(r_up) − slope(r_down) # over 30 min, only while r_up rises > 2 cm/h BI = clamp( 0.75 · s(excess; 4→34 cm) + 0.35 · s(diverge; 3→28 cm/h) ) BI = 0.7 · BI + 0.3 · camera_debris_fraction # at nodes with a camera

The learned offset absorbs calibration error between the twin and the real channel, so the twin doesn't need to be perfect. It only needs to capture how levels respond to rain.

Explain-away across the network

After an upstream blockage clears, or while it is still releasing stored water, the downstream nodes see a brief rise that resembles a blockage. If any upstream node has had BI above 0.5 in the last three hours, and the downstream camera does not confirm debris, the downstream BI is damped by 70% and attributed to the upstream node. This removed all secondary alerts in testing.

Sensor health

A fouled ultrasonic sensor tends to report a constant value. If the upstream reading varies by less than 0.1 cm over eight readings (40 minutes), the node is marked as a sensor fault, never as all clear. Its level data is excluded, the camera estimate is used where one exists, and a sensor-service task is raised.

5Risk prioritisation and alerting

hazard = clamp(rain_next_12h / 60 mm) freeboard = clamp((upstream − 30 cm) / (bank − 30 cm)) exposure = 0.6 + 0.4 · (acres / 30) risk = clamp( BI · (0.35 + 0.45·hazard + 0.35·freeboard) · exposure ) Critical ≥ 0.55 High ≥ 0.30 Watch: BI ≥ 0.30 (dashboard only)

A blockage in dry weather is High and goes to a crew. The same blockage with a heavy storm in the forecast becomes Critical. The dashboard shows each score's components in plain language, for example: "water is 31 cm above what a clear drain would show; camera sees 80% of the screen covered".

Alerts and escalation

  • High and Critical risk create a task and send an SMS plus voice alert to the village crew in Telugu, Hindi or English. The crew replies 1 to accept, which works on any feature phone.
  • A Critical task nobody accepts within 30 minutes escalates to the panchayat secretary.
  • Verified clearance sends a confirmation to the crew and the farmers on that drain.
  • Every event is written to an audit log that the panchayat can use to pay contractors for verified work.

Hydraulic model

The drain is a chain of six channel segments, each 2,000 m² of water surface with a 1.2 m bank, feeding a river outfall. Flow through each culvert follows Q = C·(1 − 0.95·b)·h^1.5, where b is the blockage fraction and C grows downstream as the channel widens. Rain runoff enters each segment from its field block (runoff coefficient 0.6). Water above the bank spills into the adjacent field. It drains back once the channel has headroom and slowly soaks in. A field counts as waterlogged at more than 3 cm of extra standing water.

Software

The prototype is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no build step and no dependencies. engine.js holds the model and detector and also runs under Node for automated scenario tests. app.js holds the loop, risk engine, alerts, workflow and UI. A guided demo pauses at each decision point for the presenter. Backup links (?scene=alert, flood, tasks, verified, impact) jump straight to each key moment.

10Market and adoption

Customers: gram panchayats (responsible for village drains), irrigation and drainage departments, FPOs and farmer groups in waterlogging-prone blocks, and maintenance contractors who need proof of work.

Revenue: a monitoring subscription per node ([₹__ per month]), installation and seasonal maintenance contracts, leasing of rake attachments at priority culverts, and verified-clearance reports for scheme audits.

Differentiation: manual patrols and standalone sensor dashboards each cover part of the job. DrainSmart covers the whole loop: it separates rain from blockage, ranks what to clear first, gets a crew moving and proves the work was done. A prior-art and competitor check is still needed before claiming novelty.

11Field pilot plan

  1. Weeks 1–2: permissions and baseline. Get panchayat consent. Identify two or three culverts that block often. Record past waterlogging through farmer interviews and any panchayat records.
  2. Weeks 3–4: install and calibrate. Mount the nodes, calibrate the twin's learned offsets on dry-weather data, and train the crews on the SMS task workflow.
  3. Monsoon season: operate and measure. Use manual clearing first. Add a rake at one culvert only after a documented safety assessment with an emergency stop and isolation.

12Constraints, limitations and next steps

  • The simulation is not the field. Real channels have uneven sections, backwater from downstream gates, and rainfall that varies across the catchment. The twin's adaptive offset absorbs some of this. Calibration is the first pilot task.
  • Camera debris estimates are simulated. The next step is a small on-device classifier trained on trash-screen photos from the pilot site.
  • Debris types vary. Silt builds up slowly and evenly, while weed mats arrive suddenly. The detector's 30-minute divergence window favours sudden blockages. A slow-trend check is planned for silting.
  • Safety and responsibility. No unattended machinery. Every clearing goes through manual override and a checklist, and the panchayat assigns who maintains shared drains.
  • Theft and vandalism. Nodes are pole-mounted. A node that stops reporting raises a service task.
